## Why collisional ring galaxies?

Collisional ring galaxies form when an intruder galaxy passes almost head-on through the disc of a target galaxy. The collision launches an expanding density wave that compresses gas into a ring of intense star formation, as in the Cartwheel galaxy shown in the JWST image at the top of this page. Their star formation has been studied extensively, but their neutral gas has been mapped in only a handful of these rare systems.

Because the collision geometry is simple and the time since the collision can be estimated, ring galaxies are a controlled experiment in how an interaction moves gas. HI extends far beyond the stars and responds to both gravity and hydrodynamics, so it records how the collision displaces gas from the disc into the circumgalactic medium and the surrounding environment.

## Why MeerKAT?

MeerKAT, the 64-dish precursor of SKA-Mid, reaches HI column densities at least an order of magnitude lower than previous observations of ring galaxies, while resolving the rings themselves. Its ~1° field of view at L-band captures each galaxy's surroundings in the same observation, including the intruder and any gas stripped into the environment. The same data deliver deep radio continuum and polarisation images, which trace star formation and magnetic fields.

## Science goals

<div class="mr-goals">
  <div class="mr-goal">
    <h4><span>1</span>Census and environment</h4>
    <p>The first deep HI census of a sample of ring galaxies and their environments. Are all ring galaxies HI-rich? How is the gas split between the ring, the interior, the outer disc, and the tails? Does the HI follow the massive star clusters, and does HI mass track star formation rate?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mr-goal">
    <h4><span>2</span>Gas kinematics</h4>
    <p>3D tilted-ring models of each disc to measure the warps, non-circular motions, and vertical structure the collision induces, and to test whether ring galaxies share common radial, vertical, and circular kinematics.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mr-goal">
    <h4><span>3</span>Anomalous gas</h4>
    <p>Separating the gas displaced by the collision from the rotating disc with Gaussian decomposition and kinematic tagging, including extraplanar gas where the resolution allows: the first homogeneous sample of splash features from a well-understood interaction.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mr-goal">
    <h4><span>4</span>Evolution</h4>
    <p>The sample spans a range of times since the collision. Do ring galaxies follow established HI scaling relations, and do disc stability and angular momentum change with time since the collision or with distance to the intruder?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mr-goal">
    <h4><span>5</span>Continuum and polarisation</h4>
    <p>Microjansky-level continuum images to map asymmetries and massive star formation, search for ageing electron populations behind the rings, look for polarised emission, and study cosmic-ray propagation in radio haloes.</p>
  </div>
</div>
